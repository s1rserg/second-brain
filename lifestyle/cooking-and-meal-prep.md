# My Approach to High-Protein Cooking and Kitchen Efficiency

## Meal Prep Over Daily Cooking
I refuse to spend 90 minutes every evening cooking dinner after a full day of engineering. Cooking daily from scratch is an operational bottleneck.
- **The Sunday Batch System:** I prep complex carbohydrates (rice, roasted sweet potatoes, quinoa) and core proteins in bulk twice a week (Sunday and Wednesday).
- Keep proteins neutral: seasoned simply with salt, black pepper, and garlic powder. Add distinct hot sauces, yogurt dressings, or herbs at mealtime to prevent taste fatigue without creating separate dishes.
- A digital food scale and airtight glass containers are non-negotiable tools. They eliminate guesswork in both portion control and fridge organization.

## Cast Iron and High-Heat Simplicity
Most home cooking is ruined by low heat and overcrowded pans.
- Steaks, chicken thighs, and firm vegetables need extreme, sustained heat to achieve a proper Maillard reaction (searing), not gentle steaming. A heavy cast-iron skillet retains thermal mass far better than non-stick Teflon.
- Stop touching the food. Put the protein on the screaming hot pan, let the crust form naturally for 3–4 minutes until it releases on its own, then flip once.
- Deglaze the fond: never waste the browned bits stuck to the pan. A splash of water, broth, or wine creates an instant pan sauce in 60 seconds with zero extra dishes.

## The Protein Density Rule
If a meal requires 45 minutes of preparation but provides less than 30 grams of protein, it is not worth the cleanup friction.
- Prioritize high-yield, low-prep sources: Greek yogurt, eggs, chicken breast, canned tuna, and whey/soy protein isolates.
- Minimize kitchen friction: cleanup happens *during* the cooking process while waiting on water to boil or pans to heat, so the sink is completely empty before you sit down to eat.
